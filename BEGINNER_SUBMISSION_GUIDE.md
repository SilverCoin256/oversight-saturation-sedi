## SECTION 11: Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | How to Avoid |
|---|---|
| Uploading PDF as manuscript | Upload .docx, not .pdf. PDF-only submissions are rejected |
| Forgetting figures | Upload all 5 figure files individually |
| Wrong article type | Select "Research Article," not "Review" or "Short Communication" |
| Missing ORCID | Create one at orcid.org before starting |
| Wrong corresponding author | Make sure your email is listed as corresponding author |
| Cover letter too generic | Use the one provided — it's written for this specific paper |
| Highlights too long | Each bullet must be under 85 characters |
| Forgetting to check PDF proof | Always review the merged PDF before final submit |

## SECTION 12: What Happens After Submission

### Day 0–5: Technical Check
Editorial office verifies all files are present and format is correct. If something is missing, they'll email you.

### Day 5–21: Editor Desk-Screen
The Editor-in-Chief or a Subject Editor reads your abstract, introduction, and cover letter. They decide:
- **Desk reject** — Paper doesn't fit or has fatal flaws (you'll get an email within 2–3 weeks)
- **Send to review** — Paper moves to peer review (you'll get an email when reviewers are invited)

### Week 4–8: Peer Review
If sent to review, 2–3 reviewers will read your paper and write reports. This takes 4–8 weeks.

### Week 8–16: Decision
You'll receive one of:
- **Accept** (rare on first submission)
- **Minor revision** (fix small things, resubmit)
- **Major revision** (significant changes needed, resubmit)
- **Reject** (paper not accepted; you can submit elsewhere)

### If You Get Revisions
Don't panic. Most papers get revisions. Read the reviewer comments carefully. Fix what they ask for. Write a response letter explaining every change. Resubmit.

### If You Get Rejected
Also don't panic. Every published author has rejections. Read the reviews. Improve the paper. Submit to another journal.
