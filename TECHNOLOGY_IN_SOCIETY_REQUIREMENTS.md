## Aims and Scope

Technology in Society is an international journal devoted to the global discourse at the intersection of technological change and social, economic, business, and philosophical transformation. It publishes scholarship that enables decision-makers to engage thoughtfully with technologies.

**Approaches accepted:**
- Scholarly/theoretical (history and philosophy of S&T, technology forecasting, economic growth, policy, ethics)
- Applied (business innovation, technology management, legal, engineering)
- Development perspectives

**Recent research directions:**
- AI ethics and AI governance
- Algorithmic accountability and fairness
- Digital divides and inclusion
- Technology and democracy / civic participation
- Emerging technologies (AI, biotech, quantum) and society
- Technology adoption and acceptance
- Privacy and surveillance
- Technology and labor / future of work
- Technology and sustainability transitions

**Source:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/technology-in-society

## Manuscript Structure

1. **Title page** — title, author names, affiliations, corresponding author email, ORCID
2. **Structured abstract** — Background, Methods, Results, Conclusions (Elsevier convention)
3. **Keywords** — 6–10 keywords
4. **Main text** — Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusion
5. **Declarations** (see below)
6. **References** — Elsevier numbered format [1], [2,3]
7. **Figures** — embedded or separate, .pdf/.tiff/.jpg, 300+ dpi
8. **Tables** — editable, not images

---

## Reference Style

- **Numbered citations** in square brackets: [1], [2,3], [4–7]
- Numbered sequentially in order of first appearance
- Reference list at end, numbered
- Format: Author names, article title, journal name, volume (issue) (year) pages. DOI URL

**Source:** Elsevier numbered reference style (standard across most Elsevier journals)

## Required Declarations

All of the following are **mandatory** at submission:

1. **Declaration of Generative AI use** — per Elsevier policy, must disclose any AI tool use in writing process
2. **Ethics Statement** — required even if no human subjects (state that no human subjects were involved)
3. **Data Availability Statement** — where data/code can be accessed
4. **CRediT Author Statement** — contributor roles for all authors
5. **Declaration of Competing Interests** — financial or personal
6. **Acknowledgments** — funding, assistance (optional but expected)
7. **Funding** — if applicable

## AI Disclosure Requirements (Elsevier Policy)

Per Elsevier policy (effective 2023, updated 2025):

- AI tools may be used for language editing, formatting, reference management
- AI tools **must not** substitute for human critical thinking, analysis, or interpretation
- AI tools **cannot** be listed as authors
- Disclosure statement **must** appear in the manuscript (typically before references)
- Statement should name the tool, describe how it was used, and confirm author reviewed all AI-assisted content
- Authors bear full responsibility for all content

**Source:** https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/the-use-of-generative-ai-and-ai-assisted-technologies-in-writing-for-elsevier

## Cover Letter Expectations

- Must establish the **technology-society contribution**
- Must name the **applied or policy implication**
- Must demonstrate awareness of the journal's interdisciplinary scope
- Should name specific policy actors who can use the findings
- Suggested reviewers with affiliations (avoid same-institution and recent co-author conflicts)

## Desk Rejection Criteria

Editors screen for three operational signals:

1. **Tech-society substance** — Is there a substantive technology-society contribution, or is this a generic social-science or CS paper?
2. **Methodological rigor** — Are methods appropriate for the claims?
3. **Applied or policy implications** — Does the paper name specific policy actors and actions?

Common desk-rejection patterns:
- Pure CS papers without technology-society framing
- Pure social-science papers without technology mechanism
- Technology impact descriptions without policy actor identification
- Missing required declarations
- PDF-only submissions
